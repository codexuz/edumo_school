Teen2Teen 2 — unit vocabulary lists (from the Workbook "Review the Vocabulary" tables) and unit focus (from the lesson plan)

Unit 1: We're wearing our new uniform!  | Family and other relationships · present continuous (affirmative, negative) · present participles
an uncle, an aunt, a child, children, a son, a daughter, a cousin, a neighbor, a teammate
Social language: ask if someone is busy; offer to call back later

Unit 2: Are you doing your homework?  | Activities at home · present continuous yes/no questions
babysit my little brother or sister, eat breakfast, eat lunch, eat dinner, help my mom or dad, play with my dog or cat, talk on the phone
Social language: talk about what you're doing

Unit 3: What are you doing this year?  | Extra-curricular activities · present continuous for actions continuing in the present; information questions
collect money for charity, learn a new language, play in the school band, play in the school orchestra, play on the soccer team, take singing lessons,
today, this morning, this week, this month, this year
Social language: greet someone after a long time

Unit 4: Are there any eggs?  | Food: count nouns · plural spelling · some / any
an apple, a banana, a bean, an egg, a mango, an onion, an orange, a peach, a pepper, a potato, a salad, a sandwich, a strawberry, a tomato,
go shopping, a cupboard, a fridge
Social language: plan a meal with someone

Unit 5: We need a box of rice.  | Drinks and more foods: non-count nouns · containers and quantities · How much / How many · simple present
coffee, juice, milk, soda, tea, water, bread, cheese, chicken, fish, meat, pasta, rice, a bottle, a box, a can, a glass, a loaf, a kilo,
have, like, need, want, delicious
Social language: discuss likes and dislikes

Unit 6: Do you eat dinner late?  | Daily routines · simple present spelling, yes/no questions · frequency adverbs
brush my hair, brush my teeth, comb my hair, come home, exercise, get dressed, get undressed, get up, go to bed, hang out with my friends, relax,
before, after, at night, in the afternoon, in the evening, in the morning, always, usually, often, sometimes, never
Social language: talk about a typical evening at home

Unit 7: How do you get there?  | Transportation · time expressions · simple present information questions
drive, go by car, ride a bike, take a bus, take a taxi, take a train, walk,
once a week, twice a week, three times a week, every day, on Fridays, on weekdays, on weekends
Social language: ask about activities and transportation

Unit 8: What do your parents do?  | Occupations · work activities · present continuous and simple present
an actor, an architect, an artist, an athlete, a chef, a computer programmer, a dancer, a doctor, a fashion designer, a flight attendant, a homemaker,
a salesperson, a singer, a student, a teacher, work at a hospital, work at a factory, work at an office, design things, fix things, help people, make things
Social language: discuss what you want to be when you grow up

Unit 9: Feel better!  | Ailments · present continuous for the future
feel dizzy, feel nauseous, have a backache, have a cold, have a fever, have a headache, have a stomachache, have the flu,
tomorrow, the day after tomorrow, this weekend, next Thursday, next weekend, next week, next month, next year
Social language: express sympathy and offer advice for an ailment

Unit 10: The key rings? I love them!  | Personal objects and souvenirs · verbs of degree · object pronouns
candy, earbuds, earrings, a key ring, a magazine, a mug, a necklace, a phone case, a ring, a T-shirt, a wrist band, gross, ugly, weird,
hate, don't like, like, love
Social language: express a negative opinion

Unit 11: Can I borrow your charger?  | Electronic devices · using electronic devices · can for permission
a battery, a camera, a charger, earbuds, a flash drive, a game console, a laptop, a mouse, an MP3 player, a phone, a remote, a TV, speakers,
charge, connect, disconnect, download, plug in, replace, turn off, turn on, unplug
Social language: ask to borrow something

Unit 12: She's smarter than me!  | Adjectives to describe people · comparative adjectives
funny, messy, neat, serious, shy, smart, strong, talkative, bad, beautiful, big, expensive, good, hot, late, nice, old, pretty, short, tall, windy
Social language: compare yourself with others in your family
